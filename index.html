03 / UNDER THE SURFACE

A model you
can question.

Useful comparisons start with visible assumptions. These results are hypothetical, not measured water savings or environmental impact.

The daily calculation

1 mm falling on 1 m² equals 1 litre. Collected water = rainfall × connected roof area × collection efficiency. Rain enters storage first; anything above capacity overflows. Then the barrel supplies the remaining daily garden target.

Garden target = max(0, daily water target − rainfall) × garden area. The same rainfall is applied to roof and garden. End storage = start storage + collected − overflow − used. Unmet target = garden target − used.

What this leaves out

No soil moisture carried between days, evaporation, first-flush diversion, leakage, seasonal plant needs, irrigation losses, hourly storm timing, water quality, costs, or local constraints. Same-day rainfall is treated as fully effective on the garden. These simplifications can overstate useful supply. An empty start keeps stored water provenance clear.

Capacity and collection efficiency are user assumptions. Input limits keep the explorer bounded; they are not installation recommendations. This is not guidance for drinking water, plumbing, structural safety, or legal compliance.

Why explore rainwater storage?

EPA WaterSense describes rain barrels as one way to collect rainfall for outdoor use, alongside reducing overwatering. Actual usefulness depends on local conditions and demand. This explorer helps compare capacity trade-offs before seeking site-specific advice.

Data, privacy & authorship

All four rainfall patterns are hand-authored synthetic examples. None represent a city, measurement, or forecast. Everything runs locally in your browser. Nothing is saved automatically; refreshing resets the demo. CSV downloads include settings, synthetic rainfall, and both daily balances.
